# Tone and evidence

Lead with the substantive answer, not gratitude boilerplate. Agree clearly when the
reviewer is correct. When clarifying, state what was ambiguous and how the manuscript
now prevents the same reading. When disagreeing, acknowledge the concern, provide
specific evidence, state the bounded conclusion, and offer a manuscript change that
improves clarity.

Never use tone to compensate for missing evidence. Avoid “as requested, we performed”
unless the analysis or experiment and its output are actually supplied. Preserve
limitations and adverse findings.
