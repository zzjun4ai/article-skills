# Concern Calibration

For each review axis, record `applicable`, `not-applicable`, or `not-assessable` before
creating findings. `Not-assessable` means the supplied package cannot support a
judgment; it is not evidence of a defect.

Create a concern only from observable manuscript evidence. Record:

- stable finding ID and primary axis;
- severity and confidence;
- faithful claim or reporting-element pointer;
- verified section, page, figure, table, or line pointer, or `location not provided`;
- observed evidence and why it weakens the manuscript's case;
- consequence for validity, interpretation, novelty, reproducibility, or readability;
- resolution test that would close the concern.

Calibrate severity by effect, not tone or repair cost:

- `fatal`: integrity, ethics, invalid design, or evidence failure makes the central case
  untenable from the supplied material;
- `major`: materially weakens a central or important inference and needs substantive
  evidence, analysis, validation, or claim moderation;
- `minor`: localized reporting or clarity issue that does not change the central
  interpretation; and
- `suggestion`: optional improvement with no required correction.

A statistical, figure, or writing issue can be major when it changes inference. A
missing detail is major only when it prevents evaluation or reproduction of a central
result. Do not manufacture findings to fill a quota, and do not infer novelty failure
without a verified prior-art basis.
